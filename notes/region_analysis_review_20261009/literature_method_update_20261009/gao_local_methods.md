## Applied terminal-field assessment protocol

1. Keep full composite neuron identity and check native/NMT node IDs, compartment types and parent topology before selecting a field. Coordinate changes are expected; topology agreement alone does not accept anatomical registration.
2. Use the full original graph to identify type-2 endings. A boundary created by cropping a region or image block is not a biological ending. Record branch points, connected target-local components, continuation across target boundaries and unresolved types separately.
3. Inspect raw native fluorescence at corresponding node positions when cached coverage exists. Show exact planes and explicitly labelled thin-slab image projections where useful for anisotropic tracing context. Record coverage, nominal sampling, contrast and local clipping. A local image projection is not the primary brain-map display or a substitute for slice-by-slice review.
4. Assign a review outcome with reasons: image-supported reconstructed arbor morphology, ambiguous ending/continuation, suspected truncation, or unavailable/unassessed image evidence. Presence of branches and leaves supports a morphology candidate; image support does not by itself prove tracing completeness, boutons or synapses. No fixed radius, minimum arbor size or expected fMRI agreement supplies acceptance.
5. Keep the small purposive pilot separate from the complete selected-neuron ledger. Report actual review coverage and unassessed fields. Do not convert this pilot into a biological prevalence estimate or a missing-as-zero terminal-strength map.

Liu-specific arbor and image-derived varicosity criteria are recorded in the [separate local Liu review](liu_local_methods.md).
