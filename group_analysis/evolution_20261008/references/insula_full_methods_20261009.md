# Insular boundaries and terminal evidence: full Methods review

Review date: 2026-10-09 (Asia/Shanghai). Scope: four specified primary papers, retrieved read-only from Zotero. Full-paper text and PDFs were cached only in temporary storage. This document paraphrases the papers; it changes no annotation, cohort, analysis or Zotero item. Attachment, extraction and page-text hashes are recorded in `insula_full_methods_20261009_evidence.json`.

The strongest conclusion is that a reconstruction leaf, a varicosity-bearing terminal arbor, a tracer-defined terminal field, and a functionally demonstrated synapse are different observations. These papers do not justify replacing one with another. They also do not establish a one-to-one correspondence between fine histological insular subdivisions and the current NMT ARM parcels.

## Evrard 2014: a histological area definition, with tentative subareas

**Exact study methods.** Ten adult cynomolgus macaques of either sex, *Macaca fascicularis* (2–7 kg), supplied the material. Forebrains were sectioned coronally, perpendicular to the AC–PC axis, at 50 or 60 µm. Alternating series were processed with Nissl cell-body staining and Gallyas myelin staining. Laminar and areal features were examined across serial sections; borders difficult to judge in the coronal plane were checked using horizontally and sagittally sectioned hemispheres. Two observers independently examined cases two or three times, compared maps, and reached consensus. An area required a width of at least 300 µm over several sections, relatively abrupt changes in multiple architectonic features, and reproducibility across both stains, serial sections and cases. This is a histological area-adoption criterion, not a 300-µm registration or coordinate-rescue tolerance. [Materials and Methods, Subjects, Histological processing, Architectonic analysis; journal pp.65–66; indexed lines 21–36.](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.23436)

The insula was bounded grossly by the superior and inferior limiting sulci. Its posterior extent ended at their junction rather than extending into the lateral sulcus. The anterior superior limiting sulcus continued beyond the limen into the posterior orbital region. Flat maps measured area widths along layer IV in granular/dysgranular cortex and along the III/V boundary in agranular cortex; widths were projected relative to the superior limiting sulcus and actual inter-section spacing. In five cases, pre-perfusion MRI helped position that sulcal reference relative to AC–PC. This was case-specific histological planimetry, not a nonlinear registration to NMT or a volumetric ARM segmentation. [Architectonic analysis and Construction of the flat maps; journal p.66; indexed lines 36–39.](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.23436)

**Evidence and limits.** Fifteen areas were reproducibly identified: four granular, four dysgranular and seven agranular. The numbered subdivisions within IDD, IDM and IDV were finer proposals. IDD1–5 were ordered dorsal to ventral, with subtle changes in layers III/V/VI, the position of layer IV and myelination. The authors explicitly lacked sufficient evidence then to decide whether the transitions represented subdivisions, repeated modules or distinct areas; some IDM transitions were ambiguous. Consequently, `IDD5` is a biologically motivated fine hypothesis, not a universal coordinate box. [Results, discussion of possible subdivisions; journal pp.79–81; indexed lines 139–153; Figs.21–26.](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.23436)

**Our adaptation.** Manual IAL/IAPM/IDD5/IDM/IDV soma labels may draw on this scheme, but their application to our individual brains needs its own native-section and laminar evidence. Conserved neighborhood relationships across cases do not prove conserved absolute template coordinates. This architectonic paper provides no graph-leaf terminal detector, bouton classifier or population single-neuron connectivity rule.

## Krockenberger 2023: independent boundary and tracer evidence

**Exact study methods.** The main analysis reused material from five adult rhesus macaques, *M. mulatta*, with prefrontal/cingulate tracer injections originally performed for other studies. BDA, lucifer yellow and fluororuby were used at 5% or 10%, with injection volumes 0.3–1.2 µl and survival approximately 14 days. Fifty-micrometre coronal sections were processed in alternating one-in-ten series for tracer revelation and Nissl; Gallyas material was available in two cases. Stereotaxic targeting was followed by examination of the actual injection core and halo. Some halos extended into neighboring areas or along a pipette track, so the intended target alone did not define the exposed source territory. [Sections 2.1 and 3.1; journal pp.2022–2023, PDF pp.4–5; indexed lines 175–214 and 273–292.](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.25571)

Initial microscope mapping examined all serial ipsilateral insular sections. A subsequent analysis selected one to four representative section series per case, scanned at 0.2 × 0.2 × 1 µm with 21 focal levels. Investigators marked cortical landmarks and inspected objects across the z-stack. In a separate analytical session, at least two examiners delineated cytoarchitecture and available myeloarchitecture without consulting the adjacent tract-labeling sections. Only afterwards were labeling and architecture overlaid. This separation is a particularly useful model for avoiding circular parcel assignment. [Section 2.2; journal pp.2022–2023, PDF pp.4–5; indexed lines 215–270.](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.25571)

**What counted as terminal evidence.** Anterograde terminal labeling consisted of stained corticocortical fibers bearing small beaded varicosities, interpreted as presumptive synaptic boutons. Each distinct varicosity-bearing fiber was charted as a continuous line. Retrogradely labeled neurons were recognized from labeled intracellular granules in the soma and proximal dendrites, with the nucleus unstained. This is a light-microscopy, morphology-supported operational definition: it is stronger than merely observing an axon in a parcel, but does not provide ultrastructural proof for each bouton. It does not define a terminal by zero children, nor give an SWC radius threshold or an automated passage-fiber exclusion algorithm. A line bearing varicosities can contain putative boutons along its course; restricting detection to graph leaves would miss such sites. [Section 3.1 and Fig.2; journal pp.2023–2024, PDF pp.5–6; indexed lines 293–314 and Fig.2 caption at line 334.](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.25571)

**Evidence and limits.** Coincidence between independently defined histological borders and labeling patches supports dysgranular stripes. The authors nevertheless note the absence of objective quantification and explicitly allow that IDD4/IDD5 or IDM2/IDM3 may be single mildly topographic entities. Their fine map is not presented as definitive. The main five-case prefrontal/cingulate sampling also does not establish every stripe's full input/output repertoire. [Section 4.4; journal pp.2039–2040, PDF pp.21–22; indexed lines 822–853.](https://doi.org/10.1002/cne.25571)

**Our adaptation.** Independent soma/parcel QC followed by projection analysis follows the logic of this study. Relabeling an uncertain soma because its projection pattern resembles a desired area would violate that logic. The paper supplies no numerical crosswalk from `Ia/Id` to IDM or IDD5 and no evidence that our current leaf counts measure its varicosity-bearing fibers. Boundary-ambiguous fine labels should support explicit sensitivity analyses, without erasing the original labels or promoting a provisional assignment.

## Chikama 1997: tracer-defined striatal fields, with a different insular scheme

**Exact study methods.** Eleven adult *M. nemestrina* or *M. mulatta* contributed retrograde experiments. WGA–HRP (40–50 nl, 4%) or dextran-conjugated lucifer yellow (20–40 nl, 10%) was pressure-injected into discrete ventral/dorsolateral striatal sites; the syringe remained for 20 minutes to limit track leakage. Physiological mapping helped target injections. Histological double staining for calbindin-D28k and tracer distinguished the calbindin-negative accumbens shell from adjacent territories. Five *M. fascicularis* contributed anterograde experiments, using stereotaxic coordinates plus physiological mapping and 100-nl injections of mixed tritiated leucine/proline into insular cortex. After about two weeks, 30-µm sections in a one-in-eight series were processed for autoradiography, exposed for ten weeks and counterstained with Nissl. [Materials and Methods, Retrograde and Anterograde tracing study; journal pp.9687–9688, PDF pp.2–3; indexed lines 21–46 and 75–148.](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.17-24-09686.1997)

**Boundary and terminal-field definitions.** Labeled somata were charted with a drawing-tube light microscope; striatal silver-grain deposits over labeled terminals were charted under dark-field microscopy. Serial Nissl sections supplied insular subdivisions according to Mesulam/Mufson's broad agranular/dysgranular/granular scheme. The authors explicitly describe the anterior-insula/posterior-orbitofrontal boundary as difficult and retain posterior orbital cortex as a separate category, while charting it alongside insula. This differs from the later fine architectonic scheme and should not be treated as its exact nomenclatural equivalent. [Charting and Fig.1; journal pp.9687–9688, PDF pp.2–3; indexed lines 47–71 and 149–169.](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.17-24-09686.1997)

**Our adaptation.** The study supports broad topographic hypotheses about insulostriatal projections; it does not supply a validated label-level expectation for all our ARM parcels. Autoradiographic terminal-field maps are population tracer observations, not counts of individual boutons or neuron reconstruction leaves. The full Methods describe labeled fibers and terminals and their charting, but provide no implementable SWC passage/terminal discriminator or ultrastructural confirmation. A corticostriatal pattern inconsistent with this paper can trigger investigation; it should not automatically change soma labels, exclude neurons, or override accepted cohort membership.

## Liu 2026: an AIC cell atlas and local functional connectivity

**Exact study methods.** This study used healthy adult *M. fascicularis*, ages 4–6 years. Its stated cohorts were seven animals for scRNA-seq, twelve for Patch-seq, and twenty for whole-cell/local-connectivity recordings; these counts are not an assertion that the cohorts are disjoint. A VEN-enriched AIC region was located using a stereotaxic atlas and checked histologically. Coronal acute slices were 300 µm thick. Transcriptomic clusters were annotated by marker genes, homology comparisons and selected layer-marker validation; L5 ET and L5/6 CT are molecular/homology-based subclass annotations rather than verified whole-brain targets for these cells. [Results, Transcriptomic landscape; PDF p.3. Methods, Animals and Surgery, PDF p.17; Cell cluster annotation and name assignment and Slice preparation, PDF p.18. Indexed lines 80–81, 1240–1242, 1255–1257 and 1264–1265.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-026-02009-4)

Biocytin-filled cells were reconstructed manually using a ×100 oil objective and Neurolucida, with the soma at the coordinate origin and the pia defining the y direction. For laminar assignment, thirty slices spanning anterior–posterior positions were assessed with fluorescent Nissl and layer-specific markers. Pia, white matter, cell density, soma size and layer markers defined boundaries; soma depth was normalized along a pia-to-white-matter path passing through the cell. This is subject/tissue-level laminar annotation, not an atlas voxel's layer designation. Morphological density maps used 20 × 20 × 300 µm bins and two-dimensional Gaussian smoothing for dendritic-field analyses. These are not parameters for NMT whole-brain axon maps or terminal density. [Biocytin staining and morphological reconstruction, Anatomical annotation, Extraction of morphological features; PDF p.21; indexed lines 1296–1302.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-026-02009-4)

**Actual synaptic evidence.** Up to eight neurons were recorded simultaneously. Presynaptic current pulses evoked action potentials while other cells were monitored for unitary excitatory/inhibitory postsynaptic potentials. Additional spontaneous/miniature event and stimulation experiments used specified pharmacology and response criteria. This provides local physiological evidence, not a whole-brain map derived from the reconstructed axon endpoints. The slicing preparation is intrinsically limited in its retained neurite extent; the Methods do not provide a whole-brain cut-end correction that we could transfer. [Electrophysiological recording; PDF p.19; indexed lines 1272–1274.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-026-02009-4)

Most decisively, the authors explicitly state that they could not directly identify subcerebral targets and that actual VEN-L/VEN-S projection patterns remain unknown despite their molecular similarity to L5 ET/L5/6 CT. They call for projection tracing. Therefore, this paper cannot validate our PT/CT/IT labels from parcel-presence rules. [Discussion, final limitation paragraph; PDF p.14; indexed line 1201.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41556-026-02009-4)

## Consequences for the current NMT ARM analysis

The actual `atlas/ARM_key_all.txt` contains `Ia/Id`, `Ig`, `Iam/Iapm`, `Ial`, `Iai` and `Iapl` labels, with both hemispheres; it does not expose an IDD1–5/IDM1–3/IDV1–2 fine map. In particular, the combined `Ia/Id` parcel cannot by itself distinguish the curated IDM, IDD5 and IDV strata. The 251637-based `Ia/Id → IDM` and `Iai → IAPM` rules in `06_harmonize_atlas_to_manual.py` are empirical project adaptations. `Ig → IG` is a recorded user decision. None is established by the four papers as a validated one-to-one anatomical crosswalk. A high empirical proportion or a software pass does not supply the missing fine-boundary evidence.

Current `neuro_tracer._mark_branch_terminals` selects zero-child nodes without a compartment criterion. `RegionAnalysisPerNeuron._soma_and_terminal_region` rounds each selected node into the atlas; `Terminal_Count` deduplicates their target-region labels. Those operations describe reconstruction topology and unique atlas targets, not the terminal fields used in Chikama or the varicosity evidence used in Krockenberger. The proposed non-root, source-type-2 zero-child definition would be a narrower **axonal reconstruction leaf** measurement. Even that proposed measurement needs tracing-completeness and cut-end QC, and is not a verified bouton or synapse. No detector was implemented in this review.

Priority next steps are to retain original soma labels and their sources; independently review native histological/laminar boundaries and template registration; record fine-parcel uncertainty including 114's provisional IDM assignment; and determine whether source image data support blinded annotation of varicosities/terminal arbors and reconstruction cut ends. Compare broad parcels and plausible fine-boundary alternatives separately. If only SWCs are available, report reconstruction leaves explicitly and keep axon passage length as a separate descriptive measurement. A distance or length threshold would be a new project method requiring validation, not a parameter supplied by these four papers.

Unsupported or unavailable evidence remains explicit: the local Evrard PDF and its unprovided supplementary figures could not be inspected; no reviewed paper establishes our ARM fine-parcel crosswalk; no reviewed Methods validates a universal soma-distance/axon-length threshold for biological terminals; and no reviewed paper makes a bare SWC leaf a verified bouton. The 2023 map and 2026 long-range cell-type interpretations carry the authors' own limitations described above.
