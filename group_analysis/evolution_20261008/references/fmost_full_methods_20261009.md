# Full Methods review: fMOST terminal fields, coordinates and cross-modal measures

Reviewed 2026-10-09. This is a source-backed methods review, not acceptance of the INS reconstructions, registration, terminal fields or existing maps. Zotero was read through its local API using the Zotero skill; no library records, settings, source data or analysis code were changed. Full text remains in task temporary storage. The accompanying `fmost_full_methods_evidence_20261009.json` records bibliographic metadata, attachment keys, content/cache hashes, exact section anchors and inspected code hashes.

## Decision for the INS workflow

The primary anatomical outcome should be **image-reviewed terminal arbor/field evidence**, with arbor skeleton length and neuron-level target presence measured separately. A non-root type-2 node without children in the complete graph is a **candidate axonal graph endpoint**. That is a useful reconstruction QC and exploratory mapping measure; it does not establish a terminal arbor, bouton or synapse. Image review must resolve weak-signal truncation, broken block joins, unresolved tracing, compartment identity and anatomical entry. Preserve all candidate endpoints and their unresolved status while this evidence is missing.

Keep total axon passage/length as a secondary measure. A passing trunk can lack a terminal field, but a branch shaft can also carry en passant boutons. Thus “passage” must not be interpreted as “no possible synapses.” Do not manufacture terminal labels by filtering the graph, choose a distance threshold merely to eliminate inconvenient endpoints, or replace the published arbor method with a convenient GNN-like heuristic.

## Gou et al., Cell 2025: exact published Methods

[Single-neuron projectomes of macaque prefrontal cortex reveal refined axon targeting and arborization](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2025.06.005), Cell 188, 3806–3822; Zotero `JWB4MYBC`, indexed attachment `8BNNG4KV`. The library page field is `3806-3822.e24`; the actual STAR Methods footers run e1–e9. Use printed section/page anchors below rather than assume the metadata page suffix describes the Methods length. The duplicate attachment `UDDF94HW` has the same recorded PDF MD5 as `8BNNG4KV`; only the latter provides indexed text. Supplemental figure legends are included in this indexed document.

* **Sampling and acquisition:** seven female cynomolgus monkeys; 2,231 PFC projectomes from 19 injection sites. STAR “Animals” (e2, indexed lines 371–373); Results lines 61–62. Imaging is 0.65 × 0.65 × 3 µm, with four optical layers per 12 µm section and acquisition from rostral to caudal (“fMOST imaging,” e3, 387–388). These are PFC data and sampling, not automatic validation or a denominator for this INS cohort.
* **Reconstruction and QC:** 3D U-Net neurite segmentation followed by neuTube skeleton extraction; collaborative proofreading corrects missed segments, extraneous connections and incorrect branches. Experts resolve difficult annotated issues and inspect major branches; cubes are sampled after proofreading and histories are logged (“Image data conversion & auto-reconstruction” and “Collaborative proofreading & quality control,” e3, 390–393). This describes reconstruction review. It does not report a held-out GNN arbor-segmentation accuracy or establish review of our exported INS leaves.
* **Registration:** manually picked landmarks initialize affine registration; B-spline nonrigid registration and U-Net conversion of fluorescence background to MRI-like contrast support concurrent registration/stitching of blocks. Cross-block connections require compatible anatomy, neurite features and no plausible alternative (“Image block stitching & atlas registration,” e4, 399–404). Results explicitly names **NMT version 2.0** (line 62). STAR describes image mapping; it does not specify SWC voxel origin, NIfTI world affine, or an XYZ/250-to-zero-based-index convention. The separate deposited-code evidence below explicitly transforms reconstructed point sets.
* **Arbor definition:** a sharp reduction in branch length and increased downstream branching density, and/or entry into cortical gray matter or a subcortical nucleus. Manually curated macaque examples train a GNN to label vertices of a **terminal dendrogram**, not individual original-tree leaves, arbor-positive or arbor-negative; postprocessing generates arbors (“Segmentation of axon arbors,” e5–e6, 437–438 and 444–446; Fig. S3A legend 539–546). Published prose says terminal-to-terminal **path distance with Ward linkage**. It supplies no numerical arbor tree-cut height, decision-probability threshold, training-set size, independent test split or quantitative held-out arbor validation. The fixed 32 clusters in e4 define neuron subtypes, not arbors.
* **Arbor summaries and spatial limits:** total length, branches, hull volume/density and root-distance features describe segmented arbors (e6, 447–455). Laminar histograms use segment-length weights and are pooled, smoothed and normalized (456–457). Arbor patches are clustered **within each specimen** because sample variability and registration error make cross-sample single-arbor overlap unreliable (“Detection of arbor patches,” e7, 463–471). This is a direct warning against treating fine voxel overlap across animals as precise arbor correspondence.
* **Different quantities in different comparisons:** “Comparison with population tracing” (e5, 435–436) compares **pooled total neurite length per cortical vertex** with electrical-stimulation fMRI activity near injection sites. Bulk STPT uses total regional neurite length against GFP volume. By contrast, “Co-innervation analysis of PFC ITi neurons” (e7, 476–477) constructs a **binary neuron × target-subdomain matrix from arbor presence**. These cannot be relabeled as the same terminal-strength measure. Statistical prose describes rank-based correlation and other neuron/arbor analyses (e8–e9, 502–509); it does not provide an animal-balanced hierarchical inference or spatial-autocorrelation null for our INS–MSTIM comparison.

MRI localization used subject T1 images plus a standard atlas (e2, 375–376). It is not an explicit protocol validating every source INS soma label. Continuous cortical depth, rather than precisely defined laminar boundaries, is used; a few samples use manually segmented native boundaries to limit registration error (e5, 426–434).

## Deposited code: stronger implementation evidence, separate from the paper

Inspected local author-code copy: `references/analysis-code_gou_etal_2025/monkeyrec`, associated by the paper with [Zenodo code record 15529826](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15529826). No live archive checksum/version equivalence was established in this review. The evidence JSON binds exact local files; `Manifest.toml` declares Julia 1.11.5. The tree has 90 files including one cache log; the 89 other inspected files contain source/configuration, not located trained weights, curated example files or generated arbor SWCs. Absence is scoped to this local copy.

**Both images and reconstructed points are transformed.** In `segreg/segreg.jl:587–597`, image output is sampled on the reference grid. `630–661` builds paired reference/sample points and sample-coordinate KD trees. `250–312` numerically solves the inverse of the reference-to-sample sampling transform. `316–402` applies that inversion to SWC points, replaces each node position with the resulting reference position, and saves warped SWCs. These positions are exported directly; `pos2idx` does not round or convert the stored SWC coordinates. Stage-2 `xformed-*` SWCs feed analysis (`segreg/0-segreg.jl:51–65`, `2-analysis.jl:32–100`); stage-3 `warped-*` output is a separate declared target (`segreg/segreg.jl:665–674`). Do not assume these stages or the portal export are byte-identical.

**The author's internal physical grid has a corner origin.** `segreg/SegReg/src/SegReg.jl:684–702` converts Julia 1-based pixel centres with `(i - 0.5) * res` and converts physical positions back using `x/res + 0.5`. `zz0atlas/src/zz0atlas.jl:207–264` loads NMTv2.0 symmetric images, CHARM and SARM with 250 µm resolution; `289–294` uses `ceil(x/res)` for integer label lookup and `(i - 0.5)*res` for voxel centres. The corresponding zero-based centre coordinate is therefore `x/res - 0.5`, not `x/res`. At exact integer `x/res`, the author's `ceil` lookup also assigns the lower cell, a boundary convention that differs from an ordinary half-open floor rule.

This **does not prove the current INS portal SWCs retain that encoding**. Local `neuron-vis/neuronVis/IONData.py:201–225` is an HTTP/cache client, not the server's transform/export implementation. The current manifest preparation explicitly declares cached XYZ/250 as zero-based NMTv2.1 centres. That declaration and in-bounds coordinates cannot settle the origin or NMT-version lineage. A 0.5 voxel shift is 125 µm per axis here; retain an explicit origin sensitivity analysis while obtaining exact source/export reference, transforms and anatomical landmarks. Do not select a convention by maximizing apparent anatomical fit. NMTv2.0 CHARM/SARM and current NMTv2.1 ARM are distinct version/parcellation bindings even when array dimensions match.

`segreg/segreg.jl:380` stores warp inversion residual `ddd[idx]` in the SWC radius field before export. This proves radius repurposing on that author code path; it does **not** establish the semantics of radii in our cached INS exports. Radius-based bouton detection is unsupported without image-derived radius provenance.

**The code and prose disagree on dendrogram linkage.** `1-rnn.jl:24–60` actually builds a terminal path-distance dendrogram with `linkage=:average`, whereas STAR e6 says Ward. Its non-root degree-one selection has no compartment filter inside that function; upstream input/label handling must be traced before treating it as our strict type-2 leaf rule. `1-rnn.jl:284–330` selects predicted class-2 clusters with at least three terminals and suppresses overlapping selected roots; `628–644` predicts by two-class argmax. This is not a fixed Ward height cutoff. `2-analysis.jl:5545–5574` requires raw per-node image-intensity metadata; `1-rnn.jl:363–394` combines lengths, root distances, volume, terminal counts, angles, intensities and derived ratios/log features. Plain seven-column SWCs are insufficient to reproduce those image features.

The curated example paths, generated train/prediction tables, and arbor SWC output paths are specified at `2-analysis.jl:5686–5719`, `5741–5757`, `5759–5781`, but those data were not located in this code copy. Hyperparameter probing includes a 90% training split (`5589–5623`); its invocation is commented at `5733`. The executed-looking final path evaluates training error after fitting all labeled examples (`5739–5754`). Neither source inspection nor those comments establish a completed independent evaluation, available pretrained model or reproducibility on INS. Obtain the authors' exact final code/model/labels and clarify the Ward/average discrepancy before faithful replication; do not silently choose one.

Soma atlas hierarchy is sampled from the mapped soma coordinate (`2-analysis.jl:1038–1057`; atlas getter `zz0atlas.jl:296–350`). The code also has a separate qroot soma-sampling path (`2-analysis.jl:1259–1282`), so an exported root coordinate cannot automatically be equated with a directly observed soma for every source category. Source category and review evidence must remain explicit.

## Fundamental comparator Methods read from the library

[Gao et al. 2022, Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01041-5), Zotero `L5TALCAJ`, attachment `IQ5H7F89`: Methods “fMOST imaging, data compression and image registration,” lines 1478–1479, explicitly applies PI-channel-derived affine and nonrigid mappings to reconstructed neurons. “Quality control and evaluation,” 1481–1482, uses two independent traces, a third merge and an independent evaluation of 600 neurons (median score 94/100). A traced branch is marked finished only after user confirmation (1480). This supports retaining image/tracer review evidence, not equating graph termination with a validated terminal site. The neuron-subtype Ward cut at 64 is unrelated to target endpoint acceptance (1484).

[Gao et al. 2023, Nature Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-023-01339-y), Zotero `SAKZIAKG`, attachment `CJUA8PUK`: Methods “Analysis of long-range axons” (1849–1850) uses regional **axon length** as projection strength. “Calculation of potential connectivity of dendrite and axon pairs” (1853) uses a spatial-density overlap model with 20 µm Gaussian smoothing and a 2 µm approach distance for *potential* synapses. This is modeled proximity, not observed synapses. “Reconstruction of dendrites and quality control” (1823–1824) excludes incomplete/abrupt-ending/low-quality dendrites and independently resolves uncertain connections. The tenfold 80% classification result elsewhere concerns dendrite classification, not Gou's arbor GNN.

[Qiu et al. 2024, Science](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adj9198), Zotero `BNB9KHB7`, main `TC8X6PWH` and supplement `ZMGEI8V8`: supplement “Single-cell reconstruction/registration” (printed p3, line 23) describes paired tracing, third-tracer sampled evaluation and applying PI/CCFv3 affine plus nonrigid mappings to neurons. “Axon projections within primary targets” (p5, line 36) requires **at least 1 mm arbor length, one terminal and one branch point** for significant arborization in a large primary target. Regions lacking terminals or branch points are treated as passing arbors; ~90% of such passing lengths were below 1 mm. Most figures use arbor length, with tip number as an alternative proxy. These are mouse hippocampal, coarse-target empirical criteria, not a transferable macaque INS voxel threshold or a synapse detector. “Axon arborization template and classification” (pp4–5, lines 31–35) uses 20-segment soma-to-tip trajectories and manually defined templates/SVM because convergent routes and soma position undermine morphology-distance classification.

## Image-based boutons are a separate observable

[Yufeng Liu et al. 2024, Nature Communications 15:10269](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-54745-6) was not located by focused Zotero queries; the open author-institution PDF was read instead. “Arbor detection and analysis” (p19) uses dense subtrees and spectral clustering. “Detect axonal varicosities” (pp19–20) combines image-derived intensity/radius peaks in 20 µm skeleton fragments, a 1.5× local-radius criterion, intensity >120/255 and duplicate removal within five highest-resolution voxels. Two experts annotate 235 image blocks for evaluation. Results (p11) reports 99% precision and 91.7% recall against 1,450 annotations, including terminaux and en passant varicosities. Discussion (p15) explicitly leaves biological validation outside scope. These image-dependent mouse criteria do not turn our SWC radii or leaves into boutons; even detected varicosities remain putative synaptic sites.

Gou's actual predecessor is **Sang Liu et al., Cell Reports 43:113590 (2024), [10.1016/j.celrep.2023.113590](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2023.113590)** (Gou ref13, indexed line 262), a different Liu paper. Focused library queries did not locate it and the publisher full-text/PDF returned HTTP403. Its abstract establishes a machine-learning separation of passing axons and terminal arbors, but its full Methods were not read here. No exact algorithm, validation metric or threshold is inferred from that abstract or substituted from a preprint.

## Concrete gates before primary terminal-field or MSTIM claims

1. Bind every INS export to exact native/reference geometry, transform stage/direction, atlas version, coordinate origin and unchanged node identity. Resolve half-voxel origin with export evidence/landmarks; record sensitivity meanwhile. Preserve candidate 251637 status and existing source labels; spatial compatibility alone cannot promote anatomy.
2. Obtain source images, image-to-SWC correspondence, tracing completion/error records and compartment provenance. Review candidate endings and arbor fields in native image context, including block boundaries, weak signal and unfinished reconstruction. Keep exclusions reason-coded and blinded to expected MSTIM agreement.
3. For an arbor-based primary outcome, obtain reviewed arbor labels or the exact author model, training examples, image features and final algorithm clarification. Validate against independent INS image annotations and report agreement/error, without selecting thresholds to fit fMRI. If unavailable, report primary terminal-field analysis as pending and keep existing graph-tip maps exploratory/QC-only.
4. Report per-neuron arbor presence and reviewed arbor length separately, then summarize within animal/subregion and weight animals equally for group descriptions. Report selected, fully assessable and unassessed denominators, injection coverage, and outside/unmapped targets. Endpoint-computable denominators are conditional reconstruction denominators; they do not establish biological zeros. Native length requires calibrated native coordinates; warped skeleton length is template-space length.
5. Compare homologous regional/surface representations only after accepted CM032/CM033 GLM and transform lineage, stimulation location/extent and common cortical coverage are documented. BOLD effect sizes describe functional responses; t-values also depend on uncertainty and sampling. Neither is a direct axonal or synaptic strength. Use descriptive association first, separate the arbor-presence and secondary length analyses, and require an appropriate spatial null and animal-level design before inferential claims. No smoothing, t-map or p-value is justified by this Methods review alone.

The current derivatives may remain useful as transparent software representations. This review does not authorize a coordinate switch, new biological exclusions, a surrogate arbor model, source mutation or real-data rerun.
